SecondAsk

A failed-payment recovery agent that prices every retry and reminder before it makes one.

It decides when and how to retry a failed payment. One logistic regression per recovery action estimates the chance it works; each action is then checked against 22 compliance rules that fail closed, so a rule that cannot be satisfied blocks the action instead of waving it through.

  • Evaluated on 12,000 simulated payments
  • Calibrated probabilities, not just rankings
  • Zero compliance violations in simulation

Research

The questions I keep coming back to are about deciding before spending: which experiment to run, which retry to make, which signal to trust.

Preprint, not peer reviewed

Forecasting Non-Additivity Before the Experiment: A Pre-Experimental Triage for Single-Cell Perturbation Modeling

Satyam1

1Department of Computer Science and Engineering, IIIT-Delhi

Abstract

Deep models for single-cell perturbation response are frequently reported to be no better, in aggregate, than a simple additive or mean-based baseline. We ask the complementary, experiment-facing question: can we predict, before running a perturbation, whether it will be saturated (an additive baseline suffices) or resistant (non-additive, worth the modeling and measurement cost)? On the genome-scale Replogle K562 essential screen the triage achieves Random Forest Spearman ρ 0.65 (95% CI [0.61, 0.69]) and ROC-AUC 0.86.

bioRxiv preprint, 20261

bioRxiv preprint, 2026 · first author

Single-cell CRISPR screens are expensive, and for many perturbations a simple additive model already explains the result. This work predicts which ones those are before the experiment is run, so compute and follow-up go to the perturbations where a deep model can actually learn something.

Quant and data

Four questions, answered with public data.

a

Macro Event Study

Code

How do CPI, payrolls, PMI and FOMC releases move markets?

Release data from FRED and prices from yfinance; reactions measured in equities, FX, rates and volatility at T+0, T+1, T+2 and one week, with significance tests across regimes and a seven-panel dashboard.

  • FOMC rate surprise: β = 0.303 on next-day equity variance (R² = 0.05). Small, but economically meaningful.
  • Long bonds buy the rumour and sell the news.
  • VIX compresses after positive surprises, most at one week.
b

LedgerLens

Code

Do company filings follow Benford’s Law?

Ten years of 10-K reports for 30 US companies in five sectors, scraped from SEC EDGAR, tested against the first-digit law, ranked by a suspicion score and written up as automated PDF audit reports.

  • Retail filings deviated most, likely because prices cluster at points such as $9.99.
c

SPLM India

Code

Can free satellite imagery anticipate revenue surprises for Indian retailers?

Sentinel-2 imagery at 10 m through Google Earth Engine, an NDBI index over the flagship stores of 12 listed retailers such as DMART, TRENT and JUBLFOOD, and a divergence engine that compares physical activity with analyst momentum. Validated point-in-time against look-ahead bias, with a test suite.

  • A research framework aimed at ACM ICAIF, not a live trading signal.
d

Oceanic Edge

Code

Does port congestion show up in ship-tracking data before it shows up in prices?

An AIS vessel pipeline that computes geofenced dwell-time congestion indices for six global ports, a backtested signal and an XGBoost model, plus YOLOv8 detection of ships and container stacks in satellite images.
